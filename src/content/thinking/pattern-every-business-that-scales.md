Opening another location adds complexity. It does not by itself show that a business can deliver its promise consistently.

This article is general educational guidance. It does not describe an employer's operating model, procedures or growth history.

## Make the promise understandable

A useful starting point is a plain sentence explaining who the business serves and what it helps them do. If that sentence is unclear, teams can make different choices while believing they are pursuing the same goal.

## Look at delivery before expansion

Ask whether the existing service is dependable, whether responsibilities are clear and whether problems can be resolved without every decision returning to one person.

These are questions for a business to investigate using its own information. They are not claims that any particular company has solved them.

## A hypothetical example

A fictional local laundry is considering a second collection point. Before signing a lease, the owner could examine whether customers receive clear collection instructions, whether orders are handled accurately and whether the existing team has capacity.

The example does not assume a profit, a growth rate or a particular software arrangement. It shows why demand and delivery need to be considered together.

## Keep the review practical

- Define the service the customer expects.
- Identify who is responsible for each part of that service.
- Review recurring problems and their causes.
- Assess capacity and costs using the business's own records.
- Decide what evidence is needed before committing to expansion.

Commercial and financial decisions need facts specific to the proposed business. A general article cannot establish whether a particular expansion is affordable or suitable.

## Preserve room for judgment

Written guidance helps teams understand expectations, but it cannot anticipate every situation. Make it clear when a team member should ask for support and who can make an exception.

A growth plan is more useful when it explains the limits of the model as well as its ambitions. Review those limits before making commitments that are difficult to reverse.
